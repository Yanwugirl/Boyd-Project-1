# Week 6 Reading and Inspiration

These notes connect the Week 6 Shared Circles exercise with a proposed two-player game poem about AI companionship and human connection. The game concept remains under discussion with Sam and Boyd. The readings guide questions and tests; they do not establish participant outcomes.

Reading scope: Loupe article; Game Poems homepage and Issue 1 introduction; LAUREN artist statement; the supplied lecture texts; selected Form+Code passages. The main text (pp. 1–22) of BST 7980 was read through the author-uploaded version; references were not independently checked. The exact Gómez-Peña assignment is still to be confirmed.

## Shared authorship and the rules of a canvas

Loupe’s company article introduces platforms for sharing, remixing and collaborative production. It offers leads for research, but its promotional claims and statistics lack supporting citations. These different activities should not all be treated as synchronous collective authorship.

Possible connection to the project: In Shared Circles, every visitor can erase a circle made by someone else. That rule raises a question about control and care. For the game poem, a useful discussion is who may alter a reply and how the other person can recover control.

Source: https://loupeart.com/blogs/posts/crowdsourcing-creativity-collaborative-digital-art-platforms

## Game Poems and a small expressive form

Game Poems presents short playable works as a form of poetic practice. Its framing supports introspection and cultural critique through interaction. The homepage and Issue 1 introduction were consulted; the individual games have not all been played.

Possible connection to the project: A three-act work can centre on one rule that changes the meaning of an exchange. The unresolved design question is what each player must do for the other, rather than how many conventional levels the work contains.

Source: https://www.gamepoems.com/

## LAUREN and the person inside an automated role

In LAUREN, Lauren McCarthy performs a human version of a domestic assistant, using connected devices to attend to participants. The artist describes tensions involving intimacy, privacy, convenience and agency, including the human labour behind automation.

Possible connection to the project: This is relevant prior work for a scene in which an apparent automated conversation contains a human participant. Our proposed distinction would need to emerge through two participants’ reciprocal choices. The reading does not establish that AI companionship is always harmful.

Source: https://get-lauren.net/LAUREN

## Embodiment and representation in XR

Weber and Cook’s 2022 article examines choreographic practice in XR through reflective accounts and workshops. The main text was read via the author-uploaded version. Participants describe sensory mismatch, touch as a grounding cue, and abstract particle bodies. Visual effects also redirect movement, forming a feedback loop between bodily action and representation.

Possible connection to the project: For our gesture interface, we should test whether the abstract body communicates an action clearly. Tracking failure needs visible feedback. We should not interpret a missing hand signal as emotional withdrawal, or assume findings from dancers in XR apply directly to a browser game.

Source: https://doi.org/10.16995/bst.7980

Author-uploaded reading copy: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/355858933_Choreographic_Encounters_in_XR_Reflections_on_Dancing_inDancing_with_the_Digital

## Form and Code as a way of designing rules

Form+Code by Casey Reas, Chandler McWilliams and LUST (2010) treats code as a creative material. Selected passages connect procedural literacy with writing systems of possible outcomes. Repeat, Transform and Parameterize provide practical ways to move from a visual intention to an executable rule.

Possible connection to the project: Repeat can organise message shapes or visual traces. Transform can make a delivered reply differ visibly from its original form. Parameterize can define an adjustable intensity or delay. These are possible experiments for discussion; they are not agreed additions to the game.

## Gomez Pena and access to digital space

A supplementary candidate is Guillermo Gómez-Peña’s The Virtual Barrio @ The Other Frontier. It challenges claims of culturally neutral cyberspace through language, access and identity. This text has been located and read, but it has not been confirmed as Boyd’s assigned article.

Possible connection to the project: A question for the project is who defines a normal relationship or an acceptable way to communicate. English-only language and an assumed ability to use a webcam can also shape participation. These are our design questions, not claims that the essay addresses contemporary AI companions.

Source: https://v2.zonezero.com/index.php?catid=5%3Aarticles&id=502%3Athe-virtual-barrio-the-other-frontier&lang=en&option=com_content&view=article

## Questions for the next discussion

- What makes the two players need each other, and what can either person refuse?
- Does the interface make a transformed message recognisable as a designed rule?
- How can the ending support human connection while retaining both players’ agency?
- What evidence would show that participants noticed and responded to each other?
